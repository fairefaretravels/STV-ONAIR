01 / OPENING

Initial Call

Start the conversation without sounding like every other wholesaler. Say your name, mention the property, and ask for a minute of their time.

Scenario 01

What to Say

Homeowner Says

"Hello? Who is this?"

Your Response

"Hi, my name is Braylen. I'm reaching out about the property at [PROPERTY ADDRESS]. I was hoping to ask you a quick question about the house. Do you have a minute?"

Confidence Tip

Say your name and the address first. A slow, clear intro sounds more confident than a fast one.

Avoid

What Not to Say

Don't immediately ask, "Are you interested in selling your house?" That turns the call into a sales pitch before you've built any rapport. Lead with who you are and why you're calling — not the ask.

02 / OBJECTION

Pushback

Don't argue when a homeowner immediately says no. Lower the pressure and leave the door open.

Scenario 02

Homeowner Objection

Homeowner Says

"I'm not interested in selling."

Confident Response

"I completely understand. I'm not trying to pressure you into anything. I just wanted to see if there was a situation where selling the property would make sense for you. If not, that's completely fine."

Confidence Tip

A calm, unbothered tone tells the homeowner you're not desperate. Desperation is what pushes people away.

Bonus

"Just Send Me Something"

Homeowner Says

"Just send me something."

Confident Response

"Sure, I can do that. What's the best way to reach you, and is there anything specific you'd like me to include?"

03 / NUMBERS

Asking for Price

Get comfortable asking what the homeowner wants before you make an offer.

Scenario 03

Question

Homeowner Says

"So how much are you going to give me for the house?"

Response

"I can definitely work up some numbers. Before I do that, I'd like to understand the property and what you're looking for. What price would make you comfortable selling it?"

Confidence Tip

Never let the fear of an awkward pause push you into a number you haven't earned yet. Ask first.

Bonus

"How Much Can You Pay?"

Homeowner Says

"How much can you pay me?"

Response

"It depends on the property and the condition. Tell me a little more about the house and what you're hoping to accomplish, and I can figure out whether we're in the same ballpark."

04 / NEXT STEP

Asking for Agreement

Transition from conversation to paperwork without sounding pushy.

Scenario 04

Transition Into Paperwork

Homeowner Says

"Okay, what happens if I agree to your offer?"

Response

"If the numbers work for both of us, the next step is putting the agreement in writing so everyone knows exactly what they're agreeing to. I'll walk you through what we're signing, and you should feel comfortable reviewing it before you sign anything."

Confidence Reminder

Paperwork feels less scary when you explain it before you present it. Narrate the next step — never ambush them with it, and never pressure anyone to sign on the spot.

05 / STAY STEADY

Intimidating Moments

Five moments that catch new callers off guard. Stay calm, answer plainly, and keep moving.

05

"How did you get my number?"

"That's a fair question. We use property and public-record information to identify properties that may fit what we're looking for. If you'd rather not receive calls from us, I completely understand."

06

"Are you an agent?"

"No, I'm not calling as your real estate agent. I'm reaching out about purchasing the property. I want to be clear about who I am and why I'm calling."

08

"Just give me your offer."

"I can give you a number, but I don't want to throw out something without understanding the property. Let me ask a couple quick questions first so the number makes sense."

07

"I need to talk to my spouse."

"Absolutely, you should both be comfortable. Would it help to go over the basic numbers together, then I follow up once you've had a chance to talk?"

12

"I already have a buyer."

"That's good — if you're comfortable with that buyer, you should consider it. If that deal doesn't work out, would you mind if I stayed in touch as a backup?"

Confidence Reminder

None of these are attacks. They're tests. Answer plainly, don't get defensive, and let your calm do the convincing.
